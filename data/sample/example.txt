The wind moved softly through the old orchard, carrying the scent of apples and damp earth. At dawn, Mara opened the wooden gate and followed the narrow path toward the river. She had walked there every morning since childhood, yet the place never seemed exactly the same. In spring, blue flowers gathered beneath the trees. In summer, the grass rose high around the stones. In autumn, the branches shone with red and gold, and in winter the river held the pale sky like a sheet of glass.

Near the water stood a small bench made by Mara's grandfather. Its boards had silvered with age, but it remained strong. She sat there with a notebook and listened to the current. The river carried leaves, twigs, and sometimes a bright feather beneath the bridge. None of these things resisted the water for long. They drifted around the bends, disappeared beneath the shadow of the bank, and emerged again in the open light.

One morning, Mara noticed a young oak growing beside the bench. It was scarcely taller than her knee, with three delicate leaves trembling at the top. She placed her hand near its stem without touching it. The tree had arrived without ceremony, carried by a bird or the wind, and had taken root in a crack between two stones. She wondered whether it would survive the summer.

Each day afterward, she brought a small cup of water from the river. Rain came as well, sometimes gently and sometimes in hard silver curtains. The oak endured. Its leaves darkened, its stem thickened, and by the end of the season a new branch had opened toward the sun. Mara understood then that a beginning did not need to look impressive. It needed only a place to grow, a little care, and enough time.

Years later, travelers would rest beneath the oak's broad shade. Children would collect its fallen leaves, and birds would build nests among its branches. Mara would still visit the river, though the bench would be nearly hidden by grass. She would look up at the tree and remember that forests, like lives, are assembled from patient and almost invisible acts.
